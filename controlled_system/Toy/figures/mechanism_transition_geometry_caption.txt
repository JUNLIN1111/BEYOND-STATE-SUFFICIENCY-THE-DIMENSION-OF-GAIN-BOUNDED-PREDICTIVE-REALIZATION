Same states, different predictive geometries. All three systems contain
the same eight distinguishable states and use a two-dimensional latent
space; only their transition families differ. The top row shows the
controlled transition structures, and the bottom row shows representative
latent geometries. Solid segments mark a current state pair and dashed
segments mark its successors under one fixed action. The cyclic transition
is realized non-expansively in two dimensions, whereas the adjacent- and
complete-transposition families require larger best-found gains. Extra
latent dimensions therefore need not encode new state information; they can
provide the geometry needed to realize the transition family.
