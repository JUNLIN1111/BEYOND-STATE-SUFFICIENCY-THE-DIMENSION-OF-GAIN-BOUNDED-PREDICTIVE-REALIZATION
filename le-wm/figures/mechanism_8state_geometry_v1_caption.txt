How dimension changes the geometry available to the same state set. All
panels contain the same eight distinguishable states; rows differ only in
the transition family and columns differ in latent dimension. In one
dimension all states must lie on a line. As dimension increases, the
encoder can realize richer pairwise geometries. The cyclic transition can
be organized non-expansively in two dimensions, while the transposition
families require more geometric freedom because their action constraints
couple many state pairs. Edge overlays indicate the controlled transition
structure; complete transpositions impose all-to-all pair constraints. For
readability, one-dimensional panels use state-ordered equal display spacing;
reported gains are recomputed from the normalized E1 embeddings.
